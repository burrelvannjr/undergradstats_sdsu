## Course Description

Statistics is a "language" we can use to describe things in our social world. We often talk about how one aspect of society relates to another — in statistics, we describe this as the relationship between two or more variables. This course provides students with the foundational skills necessary for understanding, interpreting, and drawing conclusions from the statistical analysis of data.

The course begins with descriptive and univariate statistics, including levels of measurement, central tendency, variability, the normal curve, z-scores, and confidence intervals. It then moves to bivariate techniques such as t-tests, ANOVA, chi-square, and correlation. The course concludes with multivariate techniques including ordinary least squares (OLS) regression and various model specifications.


## Course Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. Apply statistics to quantitative data to answer social science questions
2. Conduct statistical analyses using R/RStudio
3. Develop programming skills transferable to other statistical software platforms
4. Interpret statistical results and clearly communicate conclusions

## Assignments

### Course Prep & Learning Checks (50 pts)
Week 1 setup checks include verifying the email policy, setting up Google Doc submissions, installing R/RStudio, updating your course profile, posting a video introduction, and joining a portfolio group. A midterm learning check assesses comprehension of bivariate statistical tests.

### Course Engagement (50 pts)
Active attendance and participation are essential to success in this course, as in-class discussions of quantitative methodology depend on consistent engagement.

### Portfolios 1–4 (100 pts — 25 pts each)
Four short statistical projects distributed across the semester. In each portfolio, students are provided a dataset and asked to perform specified statistical techniques (by hand and/or in RStudio) and write up their findings in the style of a research report — complete with a title and all group members' names.

Students are strongly encouraged to collaborate in groups of up to three (3) and submit one portfolio per group. Portfolios are designed to serve as a lasting reference resource after the course ends.

### Extra Credit
An optional Portfolio #5 may be offered as extra credit, worth up to 10 points. It must be submitted by the stated deadline — no late submissions accepted.
